I took one end of the five-foot board, and my teammate grabbed the other. We began the arduous journey back to the door. Our third teammate explained to people why they needed to move out of the way. Once inside, the real struggle began—hauling the awkward board across the wide hallway, around the stairs, and toward the designated spot. By the time we set up the board on its easel and attached the velcro cutouts, we were already being ushered out—the presentations were about to begin. The cards handed to us as we were leaving showed that we were the twelfth team to present. With an hour and a half to spare, my teammates spent the time rehearsing. I helped here and there, but my role wasn’t to speak; I was responsible for designing the board. As we rehearsed, the anxiety that had loomed over us began to fade. Replacing it was a growing sense of confidence. We could actually win this.
